Every populated Texas ZIP code scored for hail and hurricane, with strong wind and inland (river) flooding as smaller inputs, from the FEMA National Risk Index (December 2025 release) at census-tract resolution, weighted to where each ZIP's residents live, and grouped into four tiers. This account writes Home and Auto. For a home, the tiers show where the roof and the wind and hail deductible deserve the most time on the quote; for a car, hail and flood damage is a comprehensive claim, so the tiers show where the comprehensive and deductible conversation comes up. They are a guide to the conversation, not to where the agency should or should not write. Section 04 sets out that conversation by tier, section 05 covers the four metros where your leads concentrate, and section 06 matches the tiers against the 596 GOAL leads (215 Home and 381 Auto) and 3 sales from August 12 to October 26, 2026. The full ZIP-by-ZIP list ships as a spreadsheet with this report.

03

Scoring system

Public federal data at census-tract resolution

What goes into the score
  • HailNRI hail risk: how often damaging hail falls times the building value exposed, scored 0 to 100 nationally. Half of Texas housing sits in ZIPs scoring above 88.1, so the cut points are set within Texas.
  • HurricaneNRI hurricane risk: modeled hurricane wind frequency and strength times the value exposed. Florida and the Carolinas set the top of the national scale, so Texas coastal ZIPs score in the low to mid 90s; the cut points are set for Texas.
  • Supporting inputsNRI strong wind and inland flooding risk: added to the composite score at lower weight. Strong wind matters across North and East Texas, where it lifts some mid-90s hail ZIPs into the High tier on the combined score.
  • Coastal floodingNRI coastal flooding risk is not in the composite, but a score of 90+ lifts a shoreline ZIP to at least High, so storm-surge ZIPs that FEMA's hurricane wind model scores lower are not left in Low. Tornado is in the spreadsheet only.
  • For HomeThe NRI scores the exposure of buildings, so for a home the tier is a fair guide to how often the roof and the wind and hail deductible will come into play.
  • For AutoThe NRI measures exposure of buildings, not cars, so the tiers are a guide to where hail and flood are common, which is where comprehensive coverage gets used. A car can also be parked in a garage or driven out of a storm's path, so the tie between tier and Auto claims is looser than for a home.

Tier rules
  • ExtremeHail score 98+ or hurricane score 94+
  • HighHail score 97+, hurricane score 91+, composite score 75+, or coastal flooding score 90+ (a floor for shoreline ZIPs such as Galveston Island)
  • ModerateHail score 94+, hurricane score 88+, or composite score 70+
  • LowEverything else.
  • How Extreme is setExtreme is sized to mark about one Texas home in nine (11.5% of housing). The hail arm (98+) covers 6.4% of Texas housing and the hurricane arm (94+) covers 5.1%. High and Moderate widen each arm, and the composite catches ZIPs where several perils add up.

Home and Auto: what the tiers mean

Extreme: roof, deductible and comprehensive review · High: roof and deductible check, confirm comprehensive

Extreme · full roof, deductible and comprehensive review · 396 ZIPs · 11.5% of TX housing High · roof and deductible check, confirm comprehensive · 463 ZIPs · 15.8% Moderate and Low · standard quote · 1064 ZIPs · 72.7%
The tiers are a guide to the quote conversation, and the High and Extreme tiers hold 27.3% of Texas housing. In High and Extreme ZIPs, talk through the roof and the wind and hail deductible in dollars on every home quote, and make sure comprehensive is on every car that is worth covering, with a deductible the household can meet after a storm.
Home and Auto conversation by tier
  • ExtremeHome: roof and wind and hail deductible review. Ask the roof's age and condition, and show the percentage deductible in dollars. Auto: confirm comprehensive on each car and ask where the cars are parked overnight. In coastal hurricane ZIPs, mention that flood is separate coverage for the home and that a flooded car is a comprehensive claim.
  • HighHome: roof and deductible check. Auto: confirm comprehensive on every car with a loan or real value, with a deductible that fits the budget.
  • ModerateStandard quote. Comprehensive is still worth offering anywhere hail is common.
  • LowStandard quote.
Two kinds of Extreme ZIP

Hail and hurricane in the quote conversation

Questions that make a Texas Home and Auto quote stickier

Why it matters here
  • RoofFor your 215 Home leads, the roof's age and condition and the wind and hail deductible in dollars are the first questions in hail country. A percentage deductible can be a large number after a storm, so showing it in dollars, with a second option beside it, avoids a surprise later.
  • Comprehensive40% of housing in Dallas-Fort Worth, San Antonio & South Texas, Austin & Central Texas and West Texas sits in ZIPs scoring 95+ for hail nationally. Hail damage to a car is paid only under comprehensive, so confirming it on each car belongs in the first conversation.
  • DeductiblesA comprehensive deductible the household can meet after a hailstorm avoids a surprise claim experience and builds trust. Quoting two deductible options side by side makes the trade-off clear.
  • ParkingAsking whether the cars are garaged or parked outside is a quick way to size the hail conversation for each household.
  • Flooding54% of housing in the four coastal regions sits in ZIPs scoring 88+ for hurricane, and 24.4% of Texans live in tracts FEMA rates high for inland flooding. A flooded car is a comprehensive claim; a liability-only policy leaves it uncovered.
  • BundleEvery Home lead owns a home, and 22% of your Auto leads said they do too. In High and Extreme ZIPs, the roof and the wind and hail deductible open the home quote for owner Auto leads, and the cars open the auto quote for Home leads.

Data & files

  • Risk sourceFEMA National Risk Index, census tract table, December 2025 release (hail, hurricane, strong wind, inland flooding, coastal flooding, tornado), all 6,883 Texas tracts. The December 2025 release reports river flooding as inland flooding.
  • GeographyU.S. Census Bureau 2020 ZIP Code Tabulation Areas and the 2020 ZCTA-to-tract relationship file. The file reports land-area overlaps, not population, so each tract's values are weighted into a ZIP by the tract's population times the share of its land inside the ZIP. Tract parts outside Texas are not scored. Housing counts from ACS 2020–2024. Metro county lists are in the demographic report.
  • LeadsAll 596 GOAL leads for this account, Aug 12 to Oct 26, 2026: 215 Home and 381 Auto, counted by ZIP, with the 3 leads marked sold in the agency's status files counted as sales. All 370 lead ZIPs matched a populated Texas ZIP. Three sales is a small sample; the tier splits will steady as volume builds.
  • LimitsA ZIP score describes the ZIP, not a car or a house. The roof, where a car is parked, its value and the coverage chosen decide each quote; the tiers decide where to ask first.
  • FilesReynolds-TX-ZIP-Data-10-26-26.csv · every ZIP, every score, tier and the Home and Auto conversation columns, plus the demographics used in the companion report.
